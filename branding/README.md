# Mark Renzo: five personal identity directions

Brand exploration created October 9, 2026. These are concept identities for choosing a direction before building the new portfolio. They are not five separate businesses: the public-facing name stays **Mark Renzo** in every option.

## The shared idea

A creative technologist whose curiosity turns into useful digital products. The portfolio should feel like meeting Mark: approachable, playful, and interested in what a visitor wants to build. The AI can bring projects into view, reveal a preview, and ask Mark for an answer when the knowledge base cannot help. Visitors can also browse everything directly.

Use Mark's actual photos as personal stickers. Keep everyday interface copy plain and legible. Use expressive typography for short headlines, and clear sans-serif text for answers, project stories, and controls. Stickers are accents and draggable objects, never obstacles to reading.

## 01 Curious Human

**Closest to the supplied reference.** A confident lowercase wordmark and a connected monogram sit in a quiet editorial page. Cobalt provides recognition; chartreuse appears only in small stickers.

- Palette: Cobalt `#2453FF`, paper `#F6F5EF`, ink `#20211E`, citron `#DDEF8B`.
- Type proposal: General Sans for the core interface; DM Serif Display for occasional expressive headlines.
- Personality: curious, personal, considered.
- Motion: type rises into place; photo stickers tilt gently when picked up; the AI guides attention with a smooth project reveal.
- Strength: balances professional project storytelling with playfulness.
- Tradeoff: keep layouts asymmetric enough that the familiar blue-and-paper palette stays recognizably yours.

## 02 Renzo Radio

**The most conversational.** Soft chunky letterforms and a speech-inspired symbol make asking Mark feel natural. Warm color and paper stickers lean into personality.

- Palette: Vermilion `#ED4C32`, cream `#FFF2DF`, brown `#34251E`, soft blue `#AFC8F2`.
- Type proposal: Fraunces with soft forms for headlines; DM Sans for the interface. The logo remains custom lettering.
- Personality: welcoming, expressive, sociable.
- Motion: short reply bounces, sticker peeling, and a gentle speech-tail response when the agent starts answering.
- Strength: excellent fit for a personal knowledge base and the Telegram handoff.
- Tradeoff: project pages need enough structure to preserve technical credibility.

## 03 Build Club

**The strongest maker identity.** A modular MR and condensed wordmark turn the site into a small workshop. Ticket-like stickers give the build prompt a physical feel.

- Palette: Forest `#184B38`, lime `#D4EE58`, chalk `#F4F3E8`, ink `#1D2923`.
- Type proposal: Barlow Condensed for display, DM Sans for reading.
- Personality: hands-on, energetic, resourceful.
- Motion: logo modules assemble, previews snap into position, a completed idea receives a stamp.
- Strength: strongest bridge from “what should we build?” to an actual project conversation.
- Tradeoff: use personal photography and first-person copy so it feels like Mark, not an agency.

## 04 Open Tabs

**The most exploratory.** Rounded digital typography and overlapping tab shapes make work, life, and side interests feel like parts of one personal space.

- Palette: Grape `#51449B`, lavender `#ECE9FA`, butter `#F4DC78`, ink `#302C3B`.
- Type proposal: Space Grotesk for headlines and reading; IBM Plex Mono for small functional labels.
- Personality: curious, internet-native, lightly eccentric.
- Motion: project tabs open, photos stack, draggable notes settle back into a readable arrangement.
- Strength: naturally supports the mixed personal/project knowledge base.
- Tradeoff: cap visible windows and preserve a simple mobile reading flow.

## 05 Mark in Motion

**The most expressive.** A handwritten wordmark and abbreviated MR act like a personal signature. Bold display typography contrasts with the informal marks.

- Palette: Tangerine `#F16B32`, paper `#F0F0EA`, charcoal `#242424`, pale blue `#BCCFEA`.
- Type proposal: Archivo Black for headlines, DM Sans for the interface, custom lettering for the logo.
- Personality: energetic, experimental, personal.
- Motion: the signature draws on, a short underline follows the active project, stickers respond to a drag with a little rotation.
- Strength: gives the most freedom for GSAP-driven creative storytelling.
- Tradeoff: the compact mark needs small-size refinement; keep signature text out of longer reading content.

## Suggested starting point

**01 Curious Human** most closely matches the reference and keeps substantial project stories easy to read. Bring conversational warmth into its copy, while retaining one coherent logo family and palette. Choose 02 for a warmer host personality, 03 for a builder emphasis, 04 for personal exploration, or 05 for a stronger motion-design signature.

## After a direction is selected

Refine the chosen logo into a consistent vector family, test its favicon and monochrome versions, confirm typefaces, prepare transparent photo/sticker exports, and design the interactive portfolio. The generated boards are visual explorations; they are raster artwork, not production SVG logo masters. Proposed fonts describe the intended type system and may not exactly match generated lettering. Final brand marks should receive an originality review before public launch.

# Round two

The second exploration preserves every original direction and adds five new ones plus two requested palette remixes: 05B Mark in Motion and 08B Play Mode in concept 1's colors.

## 05B Mark in Motion / Cobalt

The exact signature direction from 05, now in cobalt `#2453FF`, paper `#F6F5EF`, ink `#20211E`, and citron `#DDEF8B`. The identity board, a larger homepage mockup and a transparent logo sheet make this combination easy to compare. The homepage and project tiles are illustrative design concepts, not implemented features or screenshots of the projects.

## 06 Friendly Oddball

Sunny yellow `#F2CE38`, warm cream `#FAF7E8`, ink `#252821`, mint `#BEE1C4`. Custom marker lettering and a compact m character with eyes give the AI a friendly visual companion. Suggested interface type: DM Sans. Keep mascot movement occasional so it supports the work.

## 07 Personal Post

Brick `#B84E38`, butter `#F3DEAC`, paper `#F7F1E5`, midnight `#243D52`. A postal MR and condensed slab wordmark connect projects, travel and personal stories through a postcard format. Suggested reading face: Source Sans 3. Keep texture subtle and functional labels clear.

## 08 Play Mode

Raspberry `#DB4778`, cream `#F8F3E7`, charcoal `#2B242B`, lilac `#CABCE8`. Pixel lettering, an MR tile and keyboard stickers suggest a desktop visitors can rearrange. Suggested reading face: IBM Plex Sans. Pixel forms stay in the logo and short headings, not long project descriptions.

## 08B Play Mode / Cobalt

Only the palette is swapped: cobalt `#2453FF`, paper `#F6F5EF`, ink `#20211E` and citron `#DDEF8B`, matching 05B. The original pixel wordmark, MR, playful desktop and personal stickers carry through. Original 08 remains available. Review the new board at `concepts/08b-play-mode-cobalt.png` or open `index.html#08b`; this addition is available in the gallery while the existing presentation files retain the earlier set.

## 09 Soft Signal

Teal `#207C77`, mint `#CDE8DF`, warm white `#F8F7F1`, ink `#193C3A`, peach `#F0BA9E`. A continuous monoline mr and rounded wordmark feel calmer and conversational. Pair rounded display lettering with DM Sans. Distinctive personal content keeps the design recognizable.

## 10 Renzo Editions

Ink `#252528`, coral `#F0644C`, chalk `#FAF8F2`, periwinkle `#CCD5EE`. A high-contrast serif wordmark and compact mr ligature frame the site as a personal editorial journal. Pair the display face with General Sans for reading. Candid photography and annotations preserve approachability.
